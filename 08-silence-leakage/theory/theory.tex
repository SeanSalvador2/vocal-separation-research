\begin{abstract}
This note derives the mathematics implemented in \code{singnet/\{metrics,data,eval,
train\}/} for Direction 08: a metric (SLR) for the one failure the field-standard MSS
metrics structurally cannot measure --- vocal energy leaking into the estimate during
truly silent vocal regions (``ghost vocals'') --- and the four chunk-sampling policies
whose exposure distributions the experiment compares. The crux is the pair (\S2,\,\S3):
\S2 shows from primary-source code that museval NaNs silent frames and SI-SDR is singular
on a silent target, so no standard metric ranks two systems by ghost-vocal behavior; \S3
derives SLR with exact anchors and invariances. \S4 gives the four policies' closed-form
expected silent exposure and the leakage mechanism. Every \S3--\S5 claim is machine-checked
on synthetic signals (gate G0).
\end{abstract}

\section{The silence problem}
A vocal stem is silent for long stretches (intros, breaks, outros) where the target is zero
but the mixture is loud accompaniment. For a track with $N$ candidate chunk starts, let
$\varphi$ be the fraction whose window vocal RMS is below the silence threshold; a step
draws a start under a policy $\pi$, inducing a distribution over (chunk, activity) with
$\Prsil{\pi}$ the probability the drawn target is silent. Uniform sampling makes
$\Prsil{\pi} = \varphi$; other policies move it (\S4). The karaoke-critical failure is a
\emph{ghost vocal}: energy in $\vhat$ during a break --- most audible (no vocal masks it),
and, as \S2 shows, least measured.

\section{The measurement gap, from primary sources}

\paragraph{Silent-region identifier.} Frame the GT vocal at $100$\,ms\,/\,$50$\,ms hop; a
frame is silent iff its RMS is below $10^{\theta/20}$ ($\theta=-60$ dBFS primary, the same
constant as Direction~01's \code{sisdr} guard). Merge consecutive silent frames; keep runs
of length $\ge L_{\min}=0.5$\,s. $\Rsil$ is their union
(\code{silent\_regions}).

\paragraph{museval NaNs silent frames (verified from \code{metrics.py}).} BSS-Eval SDR is
framewise on $1$\,s windows, median over frames then tracks. The framewise guard is:
\begin{verbatim}
if not _any_source_silent(ref_slice) and not _any_source_silent(est_slice):
    ...  # compute SDR, ISR, SIR, SAR for this window
else:
    a = np.empty((4, nsrc, nsrc)); a[:] = np.nan   # SDR, ISR, SIR, SAR
\end{verbatim}
Any window where the reference (or estimate) source is silent is set to \code{NaN} for all
four metrics and dropped from the median. So museval SDR is computed \emph{only over frames
where the vocal is active}; leakage into silent regions contributes nothing.

\paragraph{SI-SDR is singular on a silent target.} With
\[
\alpha = \frac{\hat s^{\top} s}{\norm{s}^2}, \qquad
\SISDR = 10\log_{10}\frac{\norm{\alpha s}^2}{\norm{\alpha s - \hat s}^2},
\]
a silent target $s=0$ makes $\alpha = \hat s^\top s/\norm{s}^2$ divide by zero: undefined.
Nonzero leakage during silence has no well-defined SI-SDR --- the analytic reason museval
inserts \code{NaN}.

\paragraph{SAR/SIR inherit the blindness.} SDR, ISR, SIR, SAR are one BSS-Eval family on
the same frames; the guard NaNs all four together whenever the source is silent. SIR and
SAR are exactly the ``how much interference/artifact leaked'' quantities, but they are
discarded on the only frames where a ghost-vocal leak can occur. Hence no standard metric
ranks two systems by ghost-vocal behavior --- the gap SLR fills, and only it: SLR is
computed \emph{only} on $\Rsil$, the frames SDR throws away.

\section{SLR: definition, properties, limits}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \SLR = 10\log_{10}
  \frac{\sum_{t\in \Rsil}\vhat(t)^2 + \eps}{\sum_{t\in \Rsil} x(t)^2 + \eps},
  \qquad \eps = 10^{-8}\ }
\end{equation}
leaked estimate energy relative to the mixture energy in the same silent regions; lower is
better. Referencing $x$ (not the silent $v=0$) dodges the \S2 singularity. Write
$E_{\vhat}=\sum_{\Rsil}\vhat^2$, $E_x=\sum_{\Rsil}x^2$.

\paragraph{Anchors.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Do-nothing} ($\vhat=x$): $E_{\vhat}=E_x \Rightarrow \SLR = 10\log_{10}1 =
      \mathbf{0}$\,dB exactly ($\eps$ cancels).
\item \textbf{Perfect} ($\vhat=0$): $\SLR = 10\log_{10}\frac{\eps}{E_x+\eps}$ --- the $\eps$
      floor (not $-\infty$).
\item \textbf{$f$-fraction leak} ($E_{\vhat}=fE_x$): $\SLR = 10\log_{10} f$, so a $10\%$
      energy leak is $\mathbf{-10}$\,dB and $1\%$ is $-20$\,dB.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Invariances and monotonicity.} Scaling both $\vhat$ and $x$ by $c$ multiplies
numerator and denominator by $c^2$: SLR is \emph{joint-gain invariant} ($\eps=0$, exact).
$\SLR$ is strictly increasing in $E_{\vhat}$ (faithful order on leakage), and energy outside
$\Rsil$ never enters the sums. $\eps$ is a \emph{floor} on the log argument, not a
projection guard: there is no singularity to patch, since the ratio is of two nonnegative
energies.

\paragraph{$\theta/L_{\min}$ sensitivity, valid-$n$, and limits.} Lowering $\theta$ shrinks
$\Rsil$ (stricter), raising it grows $\Rsil$; SLR (and even a pairwise comparison's sign)
can move, so Direction~08 reports $\theta\in\{-50,-60,-70\}$ on every conclusion. A track
with empty $\Rsil$ is \code{NaN} and excluded (museval convention), with valid-$n$ reported.
Honest limits: SLR is timbre-blind (a $12$\,dB hi-hat leak and a $12$\,dB vocal-ghost leak
score identically), phase-invisible (sample-domain), and region-definition dependent ---
energy claims, worded as such.

\section{The four policies as exposure distributions}
Fix a track: $N$ starts, silent set $S$ with $|S|=k$, $\varphi=k/N$, and
$\rho_E=\big(\sum_{s\in S}E(s)\big)/\sum_s E(s)\approx 0$ the vocal-energy share in silent
windows. The chunk-start weights (\code{ChunkSampler.weights}) and expected silent exposure
$\Prsil{\pi}=\sum_{s\in S}w(s)$ are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Policy & Weight on start $s$ & $\Prsil{\pi}$ \\
\hline
\code{uniform} & $1/N$ & $\varphi$ \\
\code{drop} & $\mathbb{1}[E(s)\ge\theta]/(N-k)$ & $0$ \\
\code{energy} & $(1-\lambda)\frac{E(s)}{\sum E}+\frac{\lambda}{N}$ &
  $(1-\lambda)\rho_E+\lambda\varphi$ \\
\code{curriculum} & \code{energy} with $\lambda=\lambda(t)$ & time-integral (\S5) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus \code{uniform} sees silence at the base rate $\varphi$; \code{drop} with probability
\emph{exactly} $0$ (its support is $S^\complement$); \code{energy} at
$(1-\lambda)\rho_E+\lambda\varphi \ge \lambda\varphi>0$ --- the floor mass $\lambda/N$ on
each silent start guarantees the positive lower bound: silence is down-weighted, never
starved (with $\lambda=0.1$, roughly $\tfrac1{10}$ of uniform's exposure).

\paragraph{Mechanism.} The separator is a sigmoid magnitude mask; on a silent region the
correct output is $M\approx 0$. \code{drop} gives zero exposure, so the ``output silence''
mode is never fit: at test time an instrumental break is out of distribution and the model
falls back to the (nonzero) mask learned for spectrally similar content, passing
accompaniment through --- \emph{structured leakage}, not random noise (H-08b: \code{drop}
leaks more than \code{uniform}). \code{energy} keeps reduced-but-nonzero exposure while
concentrating gradient on active chunks (H-08a: quality). The plane decides whether that is
a free lunch or a tradeoff.

\section{Curriculum: schedule and exposure integral}
$\lambda(t)$ anneals linearly $1.0\to 0.1$ over the first half of training, then holds at
$0.1$ (values $[1.0,0.55,0.1,0.1,0.1]$ at $\{0,25,50,75,100\}\%$). Since
$\Pr[\text{silent}\mid\lambda]=(1-\lambda)\rho_E+\lambda\varphi$ is linear in $\lambda$,
the run-averaged exposure uses $\bar\lambda=\frac1T\int_0^T\lambda(t)\,dt$:
\begin{equation}
\bar\lambda = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{1.0+0.1}{2} + \tfrac12\cdot 0.1 = 0.275+0.05 = 0.325,
\end{equation}
so \code{curriculum} exposes silence at $\approx 0.325\varphi$ --- between \code{energy}
($0.1\varphi$) and \code{uniform} ($\varphi$) --- but front-loaded. Motivation
(easy-then-hard): fit the easy ``output $0$'' mode early (high $\lambda$), specialize on
the hard active mode late (low $\lambda$). Stated as motivation; no primary claim rests on
it (schedule$\times$LR-decay confound noted).

\section{Statistics and the selection guard}
Let $q(\pi)$ = mean best-checkpoint validation SI-SDR and $\ell(\pi)$ = mean validation SLR
($\theta=-60$). Pool $\sigma^q_{\text{seed}}$, $\sigma^\ell_{\text{seed}}$ over the three
$3$-seed cells. The jointly pre-registered tests:
\[
\text{H-08a: } q(\text{energy})-q(\text{uniform}) > \sigma^q_{\text{seed}}, \qquad
\text{H-08b: } \ell(\text{drop})-\ell(\text{uniform}) > \sigma^\ell_{\text{seed}}.
\]
The headline is the $(\SISDR,\SLR)$ plane with per-seed scatter and the do-nothing
($0$\,dB) and oracle-IRM anchors; the two axes are \textbf{not} scalarized (the tradeoff
geometry is the finding), and no cross-hypothesis correction is applied (distinct metrics,
jointly pre-registered). \textbf{Selection is SI-SDR-only}: \code{is\_new\_best} takes no
SLR argument, and \code{validation\_report} computes SLR alongside but the loop compares
only SI-SDR, so $\ell$ is the leakage of the SI-SDR-best checkpoint (unbiased for the
comparison). A test asserts the selection helper is SLR-blind; the G3 gate audits it.
